# Editorial analysis vocabulary

## Shot and framing

- **Establishing / wide:** orients geography or social space.
- **Medium / close / extreme close:** progressively privileges performance, emotion, or detail.
- **Insert:** isolates an object or action that carries information.
- **B-roll:** supporting image played over continuous speech or music. State what it contributes instead of using the label as an explanation.
- **Interview / sync:** visible speaker whose recorded speech is synchronized to picture.
- **Walking interview:** speech and forward motion combined; often adds immediacy and controlled instability.

## Camera behavior

Separate camera movement from subject movement and post-production scaling.

- **Locked-off:** composition does not intentionally move.
- **Static handheld:** framing holds but micro-jitter remains.
- **Stabilized walk:** camera travels while stabilization removes most high-frequency shake.
- **Push-in / pull-out:** camera or digital crop changes subject scale.
- **Pan / tilt:** rotational reframing.
- **Tracking:** camera translates with a subject.
- **Rack focus:** focus moves between depth planes.

Optical-flow estimates can suggest scale and translation, but lens breathing, moving subjects, parallax, and stabilization can fool them. Verify visually.

## Edit behavior

- **Hard cut:** discontinuous frame boundary.
- **Jump cut:** hard cut that preserves subject and framing while skipping time.
- **Match cut:** cut joined by shape, motion, composition, or idea.
- **L-cut:** outgoing audio continues over incoming picture.
- **J-cut:** incoming audio begins before incoming picture.
- **Dip to black:** picture reaches black briefly but the sequence continues.
- **Fade:** gradual transition to or from a uniform field.
- **Dissolve:** outgoing and incoming images overlap.
- **Montage:** compressed sequence whose meaning arises from accumulation and juxtaposition.

## Function

Name the effect in context:

- orientation;
- anticipation or tension;
- interruption;
- release;
- chaptering;
- character introduction;
- evidence or texture;
- thesis;
- contrast;
- escalation;
- callback;
- resolution.

“Cinematic” is not an analysis. Name the concrete choices producing the effect.

## Recommended report

1. Source facts and analysis range.
2. One-paragraph editorial thesis.
3. Micro timeline of the most instructive sequence.
4. Shot-family and camera-language inventory.
5. Audio arc.
6. Typography and graphic system.
7. Interview/B-roll pattern.
8. Pacing statistics and notable exceptions.
9. Transferable principles with risks.
10. Evidence limitations.
